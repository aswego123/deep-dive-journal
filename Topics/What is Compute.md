


🪝 The Hook
Someone told me "if you don't know about model compute, you don't know AI." That stung a bit — but it turns out compute is just one simple idea wearing a scary word.

⚙️ Core Mechanism
An AI model is basically a giant calculator with billions of tiny dials on it (called "parameters"). To answer a question, it does a huge pile of math using those dials.

Compute = how much math has to happen. That's the whole definition.

Two things make the pile of math bigger:
- More dials (a bigger model)
- More text the model has to learn from (more training data)

It's like cooking a banquet: one chef (one chip) could technically cook everything alone, but it would take hundreds of years. So you hire thousands of chefs (chips) and split the work. "Compute" is just the total amount of cooking that has to get done, no matter how many chefs you throw at it.

Normal computers (CPUs) do math like one person doing sums with a pencil — one at a time. AI needs GPUs instead, which are like 10,000 people each doing a tiny sum at the exact same moment. AI math (multiplying huge grids of numbers) is perfect for that "many hands, small sums" style, so GPUs crush CPUs at this specific job.
